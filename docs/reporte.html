Document Recommender System — Technical Report

TF-IDF content-based recommender & scikit-learn evaluation  ·  Author: Felix (@C0z1)  ·  November 2026

1. Description of the approach

The system is a content-based recommender built on the TF-IDF + cosine similarity approach (Path A), chosen for its simplicity and transparency: every recommendation score can be traced directly to the words the documents share. The corpus holds 33 documents across five categories — Policy/Process, Support/Incidents, FAQ, Meeting Minutes and News/Article — stored in corpus.csv with the columns id, title, text, category.

Each document's title + text is lowercased, stripped of punctuation and vectorized with TfidfVectorizer (English stop-words, unigrams and bigrams). Similarity between any two documents — or between a free-text query and every document — is the cosine of their vectors. The recommender ranks all documents and returns the Top-5, excluding the seed itself. Two input modalities are supported: a seed document ID (--id DOC011) and a free-text query (--query "...").

The results are coherent. A VPN support ticket retrieves the VPN FAQ as its top match (score 0.29), and the free-text query "I forgot my password and I am locked out" returns the account-recovery FAQ (0.41) and the password-reset ticket (0.24) — exactly the documents a help desk would reach for.

2. Evaluation summary

Quality was measured with scikit-learn, defining relevance automatically: a document is relevant when it shares the seed's category. For the seed DOC011 (VPN Disconnection) at Top-5:

MetricResultMeaning
Precision0.603 of the 5 returned documents were relevant (low noise).
Recall0.433 of the 7 relevant documents were found (partial coverage).
F1-score0.50Balanced score; good precision limited by coverage.

Confusion matrix: TP 3FP 2 FN 4TN 23. The system favours precision over recall — expected, since Top-5 cannot cover all 7 relevant documents. It returns a short, mostly-correct list rather than a wide one, which builds user trust at the cost of some missed knowledge.

3. Development areas of opportunity (improvements)

  • Semantic embeddings (Path B). TF-IDF matches words, not meaning, so synonyms ("login" vs "sign-in") and paraphrases are missed. A sentence-transformers model would raise recall.
  • Metadata & role-based filters. Adding product, recency, author and permissions would raise precision and ensure users only see documents they are allowed to access (security by design).
  • Adaptive result size. A score threshold or a tuned K would let the retrieved-set size adapt to the query instead of always returning five.
  • Hybrid retrieval & query expansion. Combining lexical (TF-IDF) and semantic signals, plus expanding queries with related terms, handles ambiguity and short documents better.
  • Human-labeled ground truth & continuous evaluation. Category-based relevance is a convenient but pessimistic yardstick (the VPN FAQ, genuinely useful, is counted as a false positive). Human labels and ongoing metric tracking would give a fairer, drift-aware measure of quality as the corpus grows.
